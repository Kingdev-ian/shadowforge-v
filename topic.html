Demand & Supply

Master how markets find their balance.

📍 Scene 1 — The Market

Imagine you wake up one morning craving avocado toast. You head to the market, wallet in hand. But overnight, a frost wiped out half the avocado farms in the country.

The vendor stares at you. "Eight dollars." Yesterday it was two. You have just witnessed the Law of Supply in action. When supply drops and demand stays the same — prices rise. The market has spoken.

📍 Scene 2 — The Shift

Now imagine everyone in your city suddenly goes on a health kick. Suddenly everyone wants avocados. Demand shoots up. But supply has not changed yet.

Farmers see the high prices and think — time to plant more avocados. Six months later supply catches up. Prices fall back down. That invisible dance between buyers and sellers finding balance — that is what economists call the Equilibrium Point.

Core Definitions

Demand
The quantity of a good consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices, all else being equal.
Supply
The quantity of a good producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various prices.
Equilibrium Price
The price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The market clears — no shortage or surplus.

The Laws

Law of Demand — as price rises, quantity demanded falls.
Law of Supply — as price rises, quantity supplied rises.
At equilibrium — quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
Shortage — price below equilibrium, demand exceeds supply.
Surplus — price above equilibrium, supply exceeds demand.

Demand Shifters

Income changes affect demand for normal goods.
Prices of related goods — substitutes and complements.
Consumer tastes and preferences.
Expectations of future prices.
Number of buyers in the market.
